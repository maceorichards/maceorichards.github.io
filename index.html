01 About

Emulating the brain in deep learning algorithms to understand computation and intelligence.

I'm a computational neuroscientist in the PhD Program in Neuroscience at Harvard, as well as a Graduate Fellow in the Kempner Institute for the Study of Natural and Artificial Intelligence. My goals involve emulating properties of the brain in artificial learning algorithms to both study biological contributions to computation and advance the NeuroAI field.

My work imposes biological features of real neurons onto artificial neural networks using modern deep learning paradigms. Such features include nonlinear synaptic integration with shunting inhibition, morphological details (configurable dendrites) shaping what a single neuron can compute, and distinct populations of neurons with stereotyped morphologies and connectivity patterns. Treating these features as variables lets me probe how they limit, enhance or otherwise constrain computation.

My other interests revolve around how such networks learn, within the context of both conventional backpropagation and bioplausible learning rules. The goals of this are to understand how these rules impact the representational geometry of different cell populations in neural circuits, and how these learned representations serve task-relevant behaviors.
